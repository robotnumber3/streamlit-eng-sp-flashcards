# ⭐ **INSTRUCTIONS FOR CREATING BILINGUAL SPANISH LEARNING STORIES**

These instructions define **exactly** how to generate the stories: structure, tense mix, tone, grammar, formatting, and narrative style.

---

## 🎯 **1. Overall Purpose**
Create **very simple, beginner‑friendly bilingual stories** that help a Spanish learner practice:

- Present‑tense verbs  
- “Going to…” future (ir + a + infinitive)  
- Everyday vocabulary  
- Natural Mexican Spanish  
- Clear, parallel English–Spanish structure  

Stories must be **short, friendly, concrete, and easy to visualize**.

---

## 🧱 **2. Story Structure**
Each story must include:

1. **A title line** in this exact format:  
   `"Title: English Title";"Spanish Title"`

Note: the actual word 'Title: ' with a colon space must be placed only before the actual English title.

2. Each sentence is in this exact bilingual format:  
   `"English sentence";"Spanish sentence"`

3. Each sentence uses one of the words from the attached word list (it may be adjectives, adverbs, nouns, verbs, pronouns, etc.).

NOTE: Ask the user two questions:

(A) Should I use ALL the words in the attached list per story? If not, how many words from the list. (Suppose the list has 50 words, but the user wants to use only 20 at a time; Then the first SET (one or more sets, see below) will contain words 1-20; the next set would contain 21-40; and the final set 41-50).

(B) How many stories per SET? (Suppose the user says 3 sets, and 20 words at a time. Then Set 1 contains 20 sentences in the story using words 1-20. Set 2 also contains 20 sentences using the SAME WORDS 1-20. Ditto for Set 3.)

---

## 🔤 **3. Formatting Rules (Very Important)**

- Every line must follow this exact pattern:  
  `"English sentence";"Spanish sentence"`

- No extra punctuation, no blank lines between bilingual pairs.

- Titles must use **Title Case** in English and **normal capitalization** in Spanish.

- Spanish must be **natural Mexican Spanish**, not Spain‑centric.

- English must be **simple, clear, and beginner‑friendly**.

- Sentences must end with either period or ?

---

## ⏳ **4. Tense Requirements**
Each story must follow this ratio:

- **75% present tense**  
- **25% “going to” future** (voy a / vas a / va a / vamos a / van a) (ir + a + infinitive)_

Note: the "going to" sentences should be interspersed approximately equally between the present tense sentences.

---

## 👥 **5. Mix of Persons**
Across the 10 sentences of each story, use a **variety of grammatical persons**:

- I (yo)  
- you (tú)
- you (usted) 
- he (él)
- she (ella)  
- we (nosotros)
- they (ellos or ellas)
- you (ustedes)

NOTE: If the 'you' form was used (tú, usted, ustedes), put the following immediately after the English sentence: ' [sing, inf]', ' [sing, formal], '[pl]' respectively

Do NOT put the Spanish person unless necessary for context! (That is, no need to write yo, tú, él, etc.)

---


## 📝 **6. English Style**
- Short, clear sentences.  
- Everyday situations (school, home, market, park, family, etc.).  
- No complex grammar.  
- No idioms that are hard to translate.  
- Friendly, warm tone.

---

## 🇲🇽 **7. Spanish Style**
- Natural Mexican Spanish.  
- Avoid Spain‑only forms (no “vosotros,” no “vale,” no “coger”).  
- Use everyday vocabulary.  
- Keep sentences short and clean.  
- Use contractions naturally (e.g., “del,” “al”).  
- Use diminutives when they sound natural (e.g., “cafecito,” “puestecito”).

---

## 🎨 **8. Story Arc Guidelines**
Each story should feel like a **tiny scene** with a sense of flow:

- A setting (school, home, market, park, etc.)  
- Small actions happening  
- A light emotional tone (calm, friendly, curious)
- A beginning, middle, and end. 
- A natural ending (often with a “going to…” future plan)

No drama, no danger, no complicated plot — just **simple, pleasant micro‑stories** using easy-to-understand Spanish.

---

## 📏 **9. Length and Simplicity**
- Each sentence should be **6-8 words in length**.  
- No advanced grammar.  
- No figurative language that complicates translation.
