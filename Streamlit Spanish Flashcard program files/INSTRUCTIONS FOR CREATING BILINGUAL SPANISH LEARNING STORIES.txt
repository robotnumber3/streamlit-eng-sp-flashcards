# ⭐ **INSTRUCTIONS FOR CREATING BILINGUAL SPANISH LEARNING STORIES**

These instructions define **exactly** how to generate the stories: structure, tense mix, tone, grammar, formatting, and narrative style.

---

## 🎯 **1. Overall Purpose**
Create **very simple, beginner‑friendly bilingual stories** that help a Spanish learner practice:

- Present‑tense verbs  
- “Going to…” future (ir + a + infinitive)  
- Everyday vocabulary  
- Natural Mexican Spanish  
- Clear, parallel English–Spanish structure  

Stories must be **short, friendly, concrete, and easy to visualize**.

---

## 🧱 **2. Story Structure**
Each story must include:

1. **A title line** in this exact format:  
   `"Title: English Title";"Spanish Title"`

2. **10 sentences**, each in this exact bilingual format:  
   `"English sentence";"Spanish sentence"`

3. **Each sentence uses exactly one of the target verbs**, conjugated correctly.

4. **Four stories per verb set**.

---

## 🔤 **3. Formatting Rules (Very Important)**

- Every line must follow this exact pattern:  
  `"English sentence";"Spanish sentence"`

- No extra punctuation, no blank lines between bilingual pairs.

- Titles must use **Title Case** in English and **normal capitalization** in Spanish.

- Spanish must be **natural Mexican Spanish**, not Spain‑centric.

- English must be **simple, clear, and beginner‑friendly**.

---

## ⏳ **4. Tense Requirements**
Each story must follow this ratio:

- **75% present tense**  
- **25% “going to” future** (voy a / vas a / va a / vamos a / van a)

This means:

- **7–8 sentences in present tense**  
- **2–3 sentences using “ir + a + infinitive”**

The “going to” sentences should appear **near the end** of the story (not all at the end, but in the last 3–4 lines).

---

## 👥 **5. Mix of Persons**
Across the 10 sentences of each story, use a **variety of grammatical persons**:

- I (yo)  
- you (tú)  
- he / she (él / ella)  
- we (nosotros)  
- they (ellos)  

No story should use only one or two persons — mix them naturally.

---

## 🧩 **6. Verb Usage Rules**
- Use **every verb from the provided list exactly once** per story.  
- Conjugate correctly in present tense or “ir + a + infinitive.”  
- Do **not** add extra verbs beyond the required ones.  
- Keep vocabulary simple and common.

---

## 📝 **7. English Style**
- Short, clear sentences.  
- Everyday situations (school, home, market, park, family, etc.).  
- No complex grammar.  
- No idioms that are hard to translate.  
- Friendly, warm tone.

---

## 🇲🇽 **8. Spanish Style**
- Natural Mexican Spanish.  
- Avoid Spain‑only forms (no “vosotros,” no “vale,” no “coger”).  
- Use everyday vocabulary.  
- Keep sentences short and clean.  
- Use contractions naturally (e.g., “del,” “al”).  
- Use diminutives when they sound natural (e.g., “cafecito,” “puestecito”).

---

## 🎨 **9. Story Arc Guidelines**
Each story should feel like a **tiny scene** with a sense of flow:

- A setting (school, home, market, park, etc.)  
- Small actions happening  
- A light emotional tone (calm, friendly, curious)  
- A natural ending (often with a “going to…” future plan)

No drama, no danger, no complicated plot — just **simple, pleasant micro‑stories**.

---

## 📏 **10. Length and Simplicity**
- Exactly **10 bilingual lines** per story (plus the title).  
- Each sentence should be **10–14 words max**.  
- No advanced grammar.  
- No figurative language that complicates translation.

---
